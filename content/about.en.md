I'm an artist-researcher from Buenos Aires, working across human-computer interaction, science and technology studies and participatory design. I work from critical disability studies and from situated knowledge, studying how bodies, biographies, practices and territories produce particular ways of knowing and of acting in the world. I make research products and speculative designs with creative technologies to ask who augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) and other voice technologies really serve, and what they could become. I work keeping a critical perspective on technology while still proposing more democratic and community-defined socio-technical futures through design.

I have specialized in voice since 2019, and I'm interested in what a voice is, what it is for, and what singing can reveal about both. Much of my current work is with people who have had a total laryngectomy, building instruments and methods in which expression and identity help navigate questions of independence, autonomy, access, empowerment and society's ableist demands. From there I study the relationships between technology, power, dignity and sovereignty: disability-led ways of doing participatory design, community governance of voice data and AI, and the political economy that decides who these technologies reach.

I also ask how art can take part in the great challenges of our time, in dialogue with science, universities, the state and society. I think of culture as a capacity to imagine livable futures and create public value, not only a sector to be funded: something that should also help define the missions and collective goals of an innovation ecosystem. This brings me back to the questions of what counts as innovation, who innovates, and what we innovate for.

I'm a PhD candidate in the Individualized Interdisciplinary PhD Program at Virginia Tech, advised by Dr. Ashley Shew. In 2015 I founded [Intercambios Transorgánicos](intercambios/index.html), an arts-science research program at MUNTREF Centro de Arte y Ciencia (UNTREF) in Buenos Aires that develops interactive interfaces for health, and I still direct it. Before that I worked as an art therapist in transdisciplinary neurorehabilitation. I trained in electronic arts, art therapy and music, and I compose and perform, most often with the Linux Laptop Orchestra (L2Ork).
