The *Laboratorio de Artes Electrónicas* (Electronic Arts Lab) was part of my work as an advisor and producer for Argentina's Ministry of Culture at Tecnópolis, the science, technology and art park in Villa Martelli, Buenos Aires. I made it together with Gala Cacchione and Mariana Albornoz.

The Lab was devoted to cutting-edge technical practices that very large numbers of people have taken up, from making video games and digital art to physical electronics and robotics. Many of the people who could take its stage were the same ones who help build most of Tecnópolis's exhibitions. The Lab gave them the leading role: a place for those creating with new media and new strategies, to inspire visitors and invite them into these worlds.

It opened in November 2021. A stage for shows and performances, with a large audiovisual system, opened onto a workshop with tables, ten screens and sixteen computers, making one large laboratory where workshops on technology sat next to performances and demonstrations of how those technologies become electronic art. Large electronic artworks held the space, while the program changed every weekend: live coding, synthesizer concerts, DJ workshops, robotics and fiber-optics workshops for young people, weekends with women and gender-diverse people in technology, and, to close the year, the *Festival de Cultura Digital*, with groups that repair "obsolete" computers, defend free software and make art from code: Cybercirujas, Flashparty, RLab and the makers of national arcade games.

In the eight days it was open during the 2021 season, 18,700 people came through the Lab, and 21,500 more during the summer evenings of early 2022: unusual numbers for the arts, which made it a real bridge between cultural production and the community. In 2022 it came back as a permanent space of the park, in three connected areas: a maker space for workshops and masterclasses, an exhibition of artists from the region, and a real-time stage for performances by established and emerging artists and students of the public universities. In October 2022 it hosted *Lupear*, a festival of digital art with audiovisual performances, installations and workshops.

The Lab became a public good within Tecnópolis's mission: bringing onto the stage the many cultural agents who work where art, science and technology meet in Argentina.
