In March 2016 I started the Inclusion program at Flexible, a laboratory of art, play, science and technology in Buenos Aires, and designed and coordinated its teaching content.

The program builds on new teaching models to value the differences within each group of children rather than smooth them out. It treats inclusion as making room for what feels different or disruptive: time and space, in each person and in the setting, to work through difficult situations together. Group artistic work, science and technology are its main tools, as ways of changing how children express themselves and relate to the world.

Inclusion was set up as an experiment and an ongoing research project. In 2018 a research grant from the Metropolitan Fund of Arts, Culture and Science, which I led with a team of two, supported the work of making the program replicable in other institutions.
