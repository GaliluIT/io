As young people face the impacts of climate change on their communities, schools need effective low-cost resources to help students comprehend large-scale processes. Shore Thing, a board game, brings sea level rise and its impacts on shorelines to the classroom while promoting understanding and acceptance.

## How it began

Shore Thing began at the a2RU Student Creative Summit in March 2024, as five students from different universities responded to that year's theme, "Play". Abby Tuckett (RISD) and I then won a 2024 Emerging Creatives Challenge Grant from a2RU for the project.

## The game

Players are coastal stewards. Working together, they move the pieces of a barrier beach from the "old shoreline" to the "new shoreline", the slow inland migration that rising seas force on these beaches, while protecting habitats and critters and keeping trash off the shore. On each turn, two dice decide an event: thermal expansion, rising sea level, ice cap melting, trash, a hurricane or habitat restoration. The boards show four barrier coasts: Rhode Island and Georgia in the United States, Mar del Plata in Argentina, and the south coast of Jamaica.

The game is cooperative by default. A facilitated mode lets a teacher or caregiver guide younger players and connect what happens on the board to real coastlines.

## Testing

Scouts aged 11 to 15 played an early version. Their feedback asked for pieces that fit together better, simpler rules, more hands-on ways to show the coastline changing, shorter rounds and a team mode. Those changes, larger pieces and more reflection cards are the next steps.
