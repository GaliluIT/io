*Naturaleza Muerta* (Still Life) is an exhibition by DOMA, an Argentine art collective that emerged from Buenos Aires street art in the late 1990s. Its objects and installations look at what advanced capitalism does to everyday life: the pressure to be what one is supposed to be, and the slow loss of what gives life meaning. The show was made over more than a year by a team of more than forty technicians, artists, photographers, assistants and producers working between Buenos Aires, São Paulo, Amsterdam and Italy.

I curated the exhibition for Sala Cronopios at the Centro Cultural Recoleta, where it ran from May to September 2018. I met and interviewed the artists, developed the curatorial concept, wrote the texts for each work and put together the exhibition catalogue, coordinating the guest contributors. The work was close to journalism: each text tied a work's concept to statistics on the social and political problems it came from, so that texts and works together denounced, and pushed to the extreme, the starkest contradictions of advanced capitalism. The texts can be read almost as a single, continuous essay: read it [in English](assets/projects/naturaleza-muerta/naturaleza-muerta-catalogue-english.pdf) or [in Spanish](assets/projects/naturaleza-muerta/naturaleza-muerta-catalogo-espanol.pdf). Those texts later became the starting point of my performance *[Esperando que vuelvan las utopías](esperando-que-vuelvan-las-utopias.html)*. In November 2018 the exhibition travelled to MAR, the contemporary art museum in Mar del Plata.

I wrote the texts in 2017, while the exhibition was being made, as a strong critical reflection on technologies and capitalist hegemony in that year. Read in sequence, from the welcome text through each of the works, they move through social media and the design of the self, data traffic and surveillance, the body as a commodity, beauty standards and patriarchy, the exploitation of childhood, and politics as spectacle, and they end with a call to break the cycle of inner slavery and to seek new collective forms of organization.

The exhibition was part of the program of Art Basel Cities: Buenos Aires in 2018. On September 9, during Art Basel Cities Week, Julián Manzelli and Orilo Blandini of DOMA and I talked about it with the curator Rodrigo Alonso inside the exhibition, in a session for Art Basel's VIP guests. Art Basel's publication on the week includes a photograph of that talk.
