*Naturaleza Muerta* (Still Life) is an exhibition by DOMA, an Argentine art collective that emerged from Buenos Aires street art in the late 1990s. Its objects and installations look at what advanced capitalism does to everyday life: the pressure to be what one is supposed to be, and the slow loss of what gives life meaning. The show was made over more than a year by a team of more than forty technicians, artists, photographers, assistants and producers working between Buenos Aires, São Paulo, Amsterdam and Italy.

I curated the exhibition for Sala Cronopios at the Centro Cultural Recoleta, where it ran from May to September 2018. I met and interviewed the artists, developed the curatorial concept, wrote the texts for each work and put together the exhibition catalogue, coordinating the guest contributors. The work was close to journalism: each text tied a work's concept to statistics on the social and political problems it came from, so that texts and works together denounced, and pushed to the extreme, the starkest contradictions of advanced capitalism. Those texts later became the starting point of my performance *[Esperando que vuelvan las utopías](esperando-que-vuelvan-las-utopias.html)*. In November 2018 the exhibition travelled to MAR, the contemporary art museum in Mar del Plata.
