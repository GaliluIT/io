After a total laryngectomy, most voice technology aims at one thing: bringing speech back to a functional standard. The Singing Machine starts from a different question. What would it take to sing?

The instrument runs in a web browser, with nothing to install. A camera tracks the shape of the mouth, a classifier recognizes which vowel is being formed, and a synthesizer sings it in real time. It needs no airflow and no vocal folds, only the gestures that shape a vowel.

I treat it as a research object as much as an instrument: a way to ask what a voice is for, and to design for expression and identity rather than only for function.
