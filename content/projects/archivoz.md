Archivoz began in 2019 as one of the experiments of [Fonocosa](fonocosa.html), from the realization that before proposing technical solutions we needed to look closely at the radical changes a voice can go through over a lifetime, especially for people going through a gender transition or a partial or total laryngectomy.

It takes the form of [a website](https://archivoz.org/) that holds a bank of diverse, local voices modeled with artificial intelligence and is used as a text-to-speech application: you can produce spoken text in any of the voices in the bank. In January 2023 we began developing categories and ways of visualizing the bank that do not sort voices by "gender" or "healthy voice / sick voice", treating the voice as a dynamic physical system and borrowing categories from physics and sound to move the voices away from the normative medical view.

Today we think of Archivoz as a community-governed voice corpus and sovereign speech infrastructure, a response to data colonialism in text-to-speech systems, drawing on the political economy of technology (Cecilia Rikap, Mariana Mazzucato). Recent technical work focuses on a corpus of about 24 hours of Argentine Spanish, built with undergraduate students from several disciplines, and how to prepare it: testing voice activity detection and dereverberation showed that aggressive speech enhancement erases part of what makes each voice recognizable, so the processing is tuned to keep vocal identity intact.

In August 2023 we presented Archivoz at Sinergias, at the CCK in Buenos Aires, an event that connects companies with Argentine health technology projects. In 2025 it received Argentina's INARIA National Artificial Intelligence Award.
