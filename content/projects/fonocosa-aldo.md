Fonocosa Aldo is one of a series of artistic experiments within [Fonocosa](fonocosa.html), built on the questions that have driven my research since 2019: what makes a voice human? What sets it apart from an artificial voice? Which aspects of the voice are part of our identity? What are the limits and possibilities of technology for reproducing the traits that make a human voice someone's own?

On one side, the work is a digital model and 3D print based on deformations of the anatomy of the human larynx. On the other, it is a sound piece that plays the voice of a person who lost their larynx and now uses esophageal speech to produce a new voice. It is a voice that is hard to listen to, and the people who use it consistently describe the social rejection they meet because of how it sounds.

The work is an exercise in imagining voice prostheses, diverse voices, and forms of sound and body that break away from the normative logic of bodies and their experiences, and it engages with the idea of embracing the monstrous.

The installation can be shown in several ways. Ideally the sculpture stands in the center of a dark room under a spotlight, with the sound surrounding the whole room; the piece lasts from the start to the end of the audio.

The sculpture was modeled in 3D by [Emilia Lopez](https://www.instagram.com/tailordrift/) and printed by [Pablo Rosi](https://www.instagram.com/tailordrift/).

The 3D object on the [home page](../index.html) is Fonocosa Aldo: you can turn it with a mouse, a finger or the arrow keys.
