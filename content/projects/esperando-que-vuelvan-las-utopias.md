*Esperando que vuelvan las utopías* (Waiting for the Utopias to Return) is a site-specific performance I made for the final exhibition of *Fronteras Suspendidas* (Suspended Borders), the yearly production clinic at MAR, the contemporary art museum of the Province of Buenos Aires in Mar del Plata. In 2018 I was one of the ten artists selected for it.

Much of the museum looks out onto the sea. In a passageway between rooms, in front of a large window, I sat on a lifeguard's chair and watched the horizon through binoculars for the whole day of the exhibition. The binoculars were made in the USSR; few visitors could read where, but they carried the utopias the title speaks of, as did the hammer-and-sickle pin on my chest and a dress in the style of the mid-twentieth century. Visitors could come close, touch the chair and take photographs.

The chair was built by a member of the lifeguards' union, one of the few who still knew how to make it. Chairs like this were banned on the beaches in the mid-1980s and replaced by booths that shelter lifeguards from the sun and wind, an improvement in their working conditions. What interested me was the lifeguard's way of waiting, more than the watching over: an active, persistent attention with no room for distraction, and a body ready at every moment to go out and care for anyone in danger. An available body, on alert.

The piece began with texts I had written that year for the catalogue of DOMA's exhibition *[Naturaleza Muerta](naturaleza-muerta.html)*, which tied each work to statistics on the social and political problems behind it. I came to the clinic with those texts and two questions: how to move from the literalness of a text to a work, and whether works of denunciation are still possible inside a museum. The answer was to leave the text almost entirely out. The title, on the wall beside the window, carries the meaning, together with a short text:

<blockquote lang="es" markdown="1">

El mercado global es traccionado con la fuerza del 67% de personas alrededor del planeta que viven por debajo de la línea de la pobreza y por los cientos de miles de personas que se encuentran atrapadas en trabajos precarizados de por vida.

Mi espera me cubre de dignidad.  
Se edifica en mi interior mientras actúo.  
No existe el silencio,  
Ahora, no estoy en silencio.  
Ahora, no estoy esperando.  
Estoy activa y disponible.  
Evocando.  
Y llamando.  
Al poder que es pura potencia en cada cuerpo sometido,  
A la inteligencia atrofiada en cada mente convertida en sub-genio,

Fuego de tal magnitud  
purifica  
cambia las direcciones de cualquier rumbo.

</blockquote>

*The global market is driven by the 67% of people around the planet who live below the poverty line, and by the hundreds of thousands trapped in precarious work for life. My waiting covers me in dignity. It builds inside me as I act. There is no silence. Now, I am not silent. Now, I am not waiting. I am active and available. Evoking. And calling. To the power that is pure potential in every subjugated body, to the intelligence atrophied in every mind turned sub-genius. A fire of such magnitude purifies, changes the direction of any course.*

The work resists the resignation that this system is the only one possible, and brings back the idea of utopias: dreams of a just and equal future worth building, even when they seem impossible. It also holds a doubt about itself: a resistance staged inside the museum, within the logic of the art system, risks being a passive one. Is it possible to resist the logic of the art market from inside it? The piece leaves that question open, as a longing, with the body as its material.
